\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This work proposed a quantitative-coupled BowTie risk analysis of the
valve train of a high-performance cylinder head conceived for the
Volkswagen AP 1.8 engine operating at 10\,000~rpm. The qualitative
BowTie diagram organises six threats and five preventive barriers
around the top event \emph{loss of follower contact between valve and
cam}, together with five consequences and five mitigative barriers. To
overcome the qualitative limitation of the classical BowTie, each
preventive barrier was associated with a deterministic indicator
(dynamic follower-contact ratio, spring natural frequency,
coil-bind margin, and Goodman fatigue factor of safety), with
the hot clearance treated as a qualitatively verified barrier
outside the deterministic layer, all anchored in
literature-based pass criteria.

For the AP 1.8 head under the operating envelope adopted, the analysis
identified two concurrent at-risk preventive barriers in the baseline
configuration: (i) the \emph{outer-spring coil-bind margin}
($M_{\mathrm{out}}=0.78$~mm against the project criterion
$M\geq 1.5$~mm); and (ii) the \emph{inner-spring fatigue Goodman
factor of safety}
($n_{f,\mathrm{in}}\approx 0.68$ against $n_{f}\geq 1$ for the
fluctuating shear loading between seat preload and full-lift force). The two findings affect different physical components and
require independent design interventions. The
\emph{outer-spring natural frequency}
($f_{n,\mathrm{out}}/f_{\mathrm{cam,max}}\approx 3.81$) emerges as a
residual concern just below the conventional 4$\times$ rule of
thumb. The remaining barriers --- dynamic follower contact
($R\geq 1.343$ in the worst case), inner-spring coil-bind margin,
and outer-spring fatigue ($n_{f,\mathrm{out}}\approx 1.27$) ---
satisfy the pass criteria with non-uniform margins. The comparison
with a conventional FMEA/RPN of the same threats showed that the
quantitative-coupled BowTie offers (i) explicit visualisation of the
causal chain, (ii) reproducibility from the sizing calculations, and
(iii) identification of multiple concurrent critical barriers via a
sensitivity analysis that a single-score RPN would have missed.

The proposed methodology is reproducible and can be transposed to other
high-performance cylinder heads operating at high rotational speed,
provided the analytical sizing of the valve train is available. The
procedure was qualitatively validated against four documented
valve-train and engine-component failure case studies from the recent
literature \citep{velezgodino2019overheadvalvetrain,soffritti2018wornvalvetrain,xing2021valvespringEAC,zhao2023camshaftV6}:
one case is caught quantitatively by the deterministic indicator set
(Xing, via the Goodman factor of safety), one is caught on the
mitigative side as designed (V\'elez Godi\~no), one is flagged
qualitatively only and motivates a candidate assembly-tolerance
preventive barrier (Soffritti), and one falls outside the present
scope and motivates a candidate lubrication-regime barrier (Zhao).
This pattern confirms the diagnostic behaviour of the procedure but
does not establish the absolute magnitude of the indicator margins
for the AP 1.8 head; an empirical campaign on a dedicated test rig
remains necessary, and its scope is specified next.

\paragraph{Validation roadmap.}
The minimum experimental verification programme that would confirm
the analytical findings of this paper for the AP 1.8 head comprises
three coordinated stages:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Spring rig --- modal verification.} A single-spring
test fixture instrumented with a non-contacting laser-displacement
sensor on the active coils is used to identify the surge response
of both springs across the 50--600~Hz band. Acceptance criterion:
the measured first surge frequency shall lie within $\pm$5\,\% of
the analytical prediction
($f_{n,\mathrm{out}}\approx 318$~Hz,
$f_{n,\mathrm{in}}\approx 571$~Hz,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fn-out}--\eqref{eq:fn-in}) under the seat-preload
boundary condition.
\item \textbf{Motored-engine speed sweep --- coil-bind and
follower-contact verification.} A motored-engine campaign from
6\,000 to 10\,000~rpm engine speed is conducted with a
non-contacting valve-position transducer sampled at 50~kHz to
resolve the deceleration peak of the cam profile. Two acceptance
criteria are applied: (a) absence of valve float at the
deceleration peak ($a_{\max,-}=20\,362$~m\,s$^{-2}$,
Section~\ref{sec:res-quant}), confirmed by continuity of the
valve-position signal with the cam profile; and (b) measurement
of the residual coil-bind margin within $\pm$0.1~mm, against the
analytical prediction $M_{\mathrm{out}}=0.78$~mm
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:Mout}) for the baseline outer spring, increasing
to the design target $M\approx 2.0$~mm after the corrective
geometric change of $H_{\mathrm{inst,ideal}}=47.72$~mm and
$L_{0,\mathrm{ideal}}=55.56$~mm prescribed by the sizing note.
\item \textbf{Inner-spring fatigue campaign --- Goodman-criterion
verification.} A representative sample of inner springs ($n\geq 5$)
is cycled under shear stress alternating
between the seat preload and the full-lift load, reproducing the
analytical mean and alternating components
$\tau_{m,\mathrm{in}}\approx 858$~MPa and
$\tau_{a,\mathrm{in}}\approx 374$~MPa. Each specimen is run to
either run-out at $10^{8}$ cycles or first-failure indication.
Acceptance criterion: the empirical Goodman factor of safety,
estimated from the fraction of specimens reaching run-out, shall
agree with the analytical prediction
$n_{f,\mathrm{in}}\approx 0.68$ within the resolution of the
sample size, thereby confirming that the baseline inner-spring
configuration is at risk and that the metallurgical or geometric
design intervention identified in Section~\ref{sec:disc-design}
is necessary.
\end{enumerate}

The total test-rig hours for this minimum programme are estimated at
the order of 600--800~h. Subsequent extensions of the work will
address (i) probabilistic Monte~Carlo propagation of the
ASTM~A877/A877M tolerances and operational variability into a
barrier-by-barrier probability of failure, (ii) extension of the
preventive set with candidate barriers on assembly tolerance and on
the lubrication regime motivated by the qualitative validation of
Section~\ref{sec:res-litvalidation}, (iii) coupled thermal-structural
analysis of the cylinder head to replace the first-order estimate of
the hot-clearance barrier, and (iv) integration of the resulting
indicator set as a constraint in the multi-objective optimisation of
the cylinder-head geometry.
