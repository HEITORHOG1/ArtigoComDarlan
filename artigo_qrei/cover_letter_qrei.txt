Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "A quantitative-coupled BowTie for
design-stage reliability assessment of a high-rpm valve train: the VW AP 1.8
cylinder head at 10,000 rpm", for consideration as a research article in
Quality and Reliability Engineering International.

CONTRIBUTION
The paper develops a quantitative-coupled BowTie procedure for design-stage
reliability assessment of high-rpm mechanical systems. Its methodological core
is the binding of each preventive barrier of the BowTie to a deterministic
engineering indicator with a literature-anchored pass/fail criterion, so that a
qualitative risk diagram becomes an auditable, numerical design-screening tool.
The procedure is demonstrated on a single, demanding case, the valve train of
a high-performance VW AP 1.8 cylinder head at 10,000 rpm, refined with a Monte
Carlo tolerance analysis (N = 2 x 10^5) under ASTM A877/A877M, and contrasted
with the conventional FMEA/RPN prioritisation, whose loss of resolution around a
single high-severity top event we quantify.

FIT WITH THE JOURNAL
We believe the work is well aligned with methodological contributions recently
published in QREI, including the BOBTA bow-tie extension (Hellas & Khabtane,
2026; doi:10.1002/qre.70069), the probabilistic uncertain-linguistic FMEA
approach (Tang, 2025; doi:10.1002/qre.3657), and the Monte-Carlo
structural-reliability analysis of a single load-bearing component reported by
Qian et al. (2023; doi:10.1002/qre.3293).

ON THE SCOPE OF THE EVIDENCE (stated openly)
The study is analytical and based on a single design instance. We do not claim
experimental validation, and we address this directly: the deterministic
indicators are checked for face validity against four published valve-train
failure investigations, the probabilistic robustness of the critical findings is
established by Monte Carlo propagation of manufacturing tolerances, and the
Monte Carlo script is provided as supplementary material for full
reproducibility. A test-rig validation programme with statistical acceptance
criteria is specified as declared future work. We believe the methodological
contribution, a reproducible, indicator-driven barrier check for design-stage
reliability screening, stands on this basis, and that its transparent
treatment of the empirical gap is appropriate for a design-stage reliability
method.
