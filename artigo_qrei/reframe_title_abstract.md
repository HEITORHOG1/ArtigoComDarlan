## Abstract (~215 palavras — dentro do limite QREI de 230)

Pushing a spark-ignition engine to 10,000 rpm makes the valve train load-critical: inertial
demand, spring dynamics, and head thermal management become tightly coupled, and small departures
from the design envelope can cascade into valve–piston collision. This paper develops a
quantitative-coupled BowTie procedure for design-stage reliability screening of high-rpm valve
trains and demonstrates it on a high-performance VW AP 1.8 cylinder head. Threats, preventive
barriers, consequences, and mitigative barriers are organised around the top event "loss of
follower contact between valve and cam", and each preventive barrier is bound to a deterministic
indicator with a literature-anchored pass criterion, derived from the analytical sizing of the
intake valve and the dual chrome–silicon springs. All four dynamic-ratio indicators meet the
project criterion (R ≥ 1.20; worst case 1.343), but two concurrent at-risk barriers emerge: the
outer-spring coil-bind margin (0.78 mm against 1.5 mm) and the inner-spring Goodman factor of
safety (≈ 0.68 against 1). A Monte Carlo refinement under ASTM A877/A877M tolerances
(N = 2 × 10^5) confirms the inner-spring Goodman deficit across the whole manufactured population
and reclassifies the inner-spring coil-bind margin from active to marginal. The procedure is
checked for face validity against four published valve-train failure investigations. The study is
analytical and single-case; experimental validation is left as declared future work, for which a
test-rig programme with statistical acceptance criteria is specified.
