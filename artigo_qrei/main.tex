\begin{abstract}
Design-stage reliability screening is hardest when a failure has many
contributing barriers and no field data yet exists. This paper introduces the
Quantitative-Coupled BowTie (QC-BowTie): each preventive barrier of a BowTie
diagram is bound to a deterministic engineering indicator with an explicit pass
criterion, and manufacturing tolerances are then propagated through those
indicators by Monte Carlo, so that every barrier is reported as an
\emph{active}, \emph{marginal}, or \emph{at-risk} probability of compliance
failure. The deliverable is the method; a high-rpm valve train, a VW AP 1.8
cylinder head at 10\,000~rpm, is the demonstrator. Deterministic evaluation
flags two at-risk barriers: the outer-spring coil-bind margin and the
inner-spring Goodman factor of safety (about 0.68). A Monte Carlo refinement
($N=2\times 10^{5}$, ASTM~A877/A877M tolerances) confirms the Goodman deficit
across the whole manufactured population and exposes a
\emph{marginal} inner-spring coil-bind barrier (16.2\,\% compliance-failure
probability) that the deterministic layer reports as safe, a distinction a
conventional FMEA/RPN ranking blurs. The method is checked for construct
validity against four published valve-train failure investigations. Being
analytical and single-case, it leaves experimental validation as declared
future work, for which a test-rig programme is specified. The QC-BowTie
converts a qualitative risk diagram into an auditable, probabilistic
design-screening tool transferable to other high-load mechanical systems.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Nomenclature and glossary}
\noindent\emph{Valve-train terms.}
\begin{description}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item[Loss of follower contact (valve float)] separation of the cam follower from the cam profile at high speed, when spring force can no longer sustain the follower's inertial demand; the top event of the BowTie.
\item[Coil bind] the condition in which a helical compression spring is fully compressed and adjacent coils touch, so no further travel is possible.
\item[Surge / spring natural frequency $f_{n}$] the resonant axial standing wave of a valve spring; if a cam harmonic coincides with $f_{n}$ the spring loses effective stiffness.
\item[Cam harmonics] the Fourier components of the periodic cam-lift excitation ($n\,f_{\mathrm{cam}}$).
\item[Goodman criterion / factor of safety $n_{f}$] an alternating--mean fatigue safety factor for a component under combined mean and cyclic stress.
\item[Wahl factor $K_{w}$] the curvature stress-correction factor for a helical spring.
\end{description}
\noindent\emph{Method indicators.}
\begin{description}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item[$R$] dynamic follower-contact ratio: critical spring force divided by critical inertial force (pass if $R\geq$ target).
\item[$M$] coil-bind margin: spring height at full lift minus solid height.
\item[{$P[\mathrm{fail}]$}] compliance-failure probability: the Monte Carlo probability that a barrier indicator misses its pass criterion under manufacturing tolerance. Barrier status: \emph{active} ($P[\mathrm{fail}]\leq 10\,\%$), \emph{marginal} ($10\,\%<P[\mathrm{fail}]\leq 50\,\%$), \emph{at-risk} ($P[\mathrm{fail}]>50\,\%$).
\end{description}
